## What the upstream receipt APIs can support

At WebView main, `SwiftUIWebView.swift::WebViewMessage` (1375 onward) captures immutable frame provenance and `javaScriptBindingToken`. Dispatch reserves the native sequence and captures receipt evidence before asynchronous handler scheduling (4671 onward). `scheduleDocumentMessageHandler` (3510 onward) rejects obsolete contexts after its initial yield, scopes evidence to the handler task, and cancellation invalidates pending contexts. This is correctly ported framework-level dispatch fencing. A receipt sequence orders messages; it is not durable mutation authority.

`currentJavaScriptBindingToken` (7394 onward) includes caller identity, readiness generation and document generation; unavailable identity returns nil. `makeJavaScriptBindingCommitFence(requiring:)` (7420 onward) checks the captured token on MainActor and returns a Sendable, lock-backed revocation predicate. Binding/context/coordinator retirement revokes it; caller release also makes it false. This allows a background Realm closure to check the captured document's continued authority without accessing WebKit from that actor. Bound JavaScript evaluation also checks the required binding before and after evaluation. The fence is a revocation predicate, not a complete business authorization policy or an atomic lock spanning an entire Realm transaction.

Pending WebView #23 preserves that mechanism and adds handler-scoped evidence providers (284 onward), immutable native document URL and strictly typed BFCache-restoration hints at receipt capture (4750–4770), plus verified native URL-transition intent. It snapshots handler registration before provider reentry (4712 onward); scoped provider collisions withdraw evidence rather than selecting a winner. These additions can associate immutable app evidence with the correct handler and reconcile SPA/history URL changes. A false restoration hint must never grant authority. They do not automatically choose a Lake Realm record or add a writer predicate.

Pending Lake #50 demonstrates the stronger pattern only for EPUB initialization: it captures the exact receipt binding, requires main-frame provenance, and passes it through `Reader/Books/ReaderEBookInitialization.swift` validations and bound evaluation. The coordinator rechecks cancellation/current binding around register, acknowledgement, preparation, restoration and publication. That is relevant architectural evidence, not coverage for metadata, image, RSS, or cached readability writes. Preserve #50's initialization and import identity when adapting other paths.

The remaining cross-boundary contract is explicit: capture immutable target URL/record identity and exact native binding before the first suspension; reject unavailable identity rather than sampling a successor; establish native provenance for each message type; carry revocable authority through any separate render task; check it with cancellation inside the final Realm mutation closure before changing fields or refresh metadata; use the same captured token for frame evaluation/navigation effects. App render generations, durable record identity and document identity must remain separate. Related-record propagation additionally needs a defined policy for which records an admitted document may modify. Preventing an entire multi-record operation from partially committing after revocation would require a further transaction/ownership contract; the existing fence alone does not promise that.
